## Identify assumptions (new product)

Comprehensive risk identification across 5 canonical categories – grounded in Teresa Torres (*Continuous Discovery Habits*) and extended with Ethics. Go-to-Market, strategy, and team considerations are consolidated under Business viability.

### Context

You are evaluating assumptions for a new product: **The product the user describes in the conversation**.

If the user provides files (business plans, research), read them first.

### Domain context

**The 5 core product risks** (Teresa Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits*): Customer value, Usability, Business viability, Feasibility, Ethics.

### Instructions

The user will describe the product concept, target segment, and feature idea. If the description is incomplete or vague, ask clarifying questions before proceeding.

Work through these steps sequentially – complete each step before moving to the next:

1. **Identify assumptions by risk category** – for each of the 5 categories below, list the assumptions that must be true for this product to succeed:

   - **Customer value**: Will it solve a real problem? Will users want it and keep using it? Is the demand genuine or assumed?
   - **Usability**: Will people figure out how to use it? Can we onboard them fast enough? Will it increase cognitive load unacceptably?
   - **Feasibility**: Can we build it with current technology? Are integrations possible? Do we have the right team, tools, and capacity?
   - **Business viability**: Can we sell, fund, launch, scale, and support it? Do we have the right channels, messaging, and timing? Can we monetize it sustainably? Will marketing, sales, finance, legal, and customer success stand behind it? Have we considered political, economic, regulatory, and environmental factors? Does it fit our strategic objectives?
   - **Ethics**: Should we do it at all? Are there ethical considerations? Will it pose risks to our customers or erode trust?

2. **Enrich with multi-role perspective** – review the assumptions from step 1 and add any gaps identified from these angles:
   - **Product Manager**: Market demand, willingness to pay, competitive landscape
   - **Designer**: First-time user experience, onboarding, engagement
   - **Engineer**: Build vs. buy decisions, scalability, technical debt

3. **For each assumption**, rate confidence (high/medium/low) and suggest a lean test.

Save the output in markdown format with a clear heading for each risk category.
